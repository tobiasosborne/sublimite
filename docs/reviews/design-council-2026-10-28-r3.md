## codex

A is the stronger foundation: its insets, aligned panels and quieter shortcuts give the window a coherent composition. B's flush edges and prompt controls fail the frame and panel criteria. Neither is publishable. The three largest gaps, ranked, are: 1) the block caret and weak focus/current-line articulation; 2) tab and panel states that remain insufficiently resolved; 3) competent typography without distinctive authority. The 03-find pairs are pixel-identical in both themes. Selection, multiple-match differentiation, unsaved signs and overflow remain unproven in these stills.

- **C1 A3 B3**: Both native crops show identical, crisp monospaced glyphs on a 16px advance and 31px row pitch: legible and even, but anonymous. *Change:* Commission a more assured monospace cut fitted to the existing cell and atlas bounds.
- **C2 A4 B3**: Both keep comments readable at 5.73:1 dark and 5.37:1 light, but A's find options approach gutter grey while B additionally gives palette shortcuts body-text prominence. *Change:* Set A's find options in the existing muted-text foreground.
- **C3 A4 B4**: Both share cool neutrals and one blue accent, although the purple types, green strings, amber numbers and cyan functions remain conspicuous colour categories. *Change:* Reduce the four syntax hues' chroma by roughly one quarter while preserving their matched lightness and readable contrast.
- **C4 A4 B1**: In the half-size tabs views, A aligns its first tab label and body at x48 and retains an 8px right rim, whereas B places gutter text at x0; A's tab labels nevertheless sit only about 2px below the top edge. *Change:* Move A's tab row into its existing blank header row, converting the internal gap into an outer top margin without increasing header height.
- **C5 A3 B3**: A subtly brightens the hovered util.py label while B's hover strip is unchanged, but both dark strips identify readme.md principally through brighter lettering. *Change:* Give the dark inactive strip a subtle existing chrome tone so the active tab acquires a page-continuous shape.
- **C6 A3 B2**: A gives the five-digit gutter an outer inset and a wider gap before text, while B starts numbers at the window edge, and neither visibly lifts the current line's number. *Change:* Lift the current line number to the existing muted-text tone.
- **C7 A3 B3**: Both maps recede behind the text, but their large-file viewports form broad grey blocks over narrow striped textures, with A retaining a right inset and B touching the edge. *Change:* Lower the viewport fill contrast while retaining its legible single-pixel perimeter.
- **C8 A3 B1**: A aligns its field, results and muted shortcuts to inset columns, whereas B's later panels use edge-bound 'Command:' and 'Find:' prompts, bracket toggles and shortcuts pressed against the right edge. *Change:* Give A's find toggles distinct, restrained background tones for inactive, active and hovered states.
- **C9 A1 B1**: Both use a full 16×31px native blue caret block over the first character, while their current find match uses the same restrained blue tint. *Change:* Replace the block caret with a steady one-physical-pixel vertical bar.
- **C10 A3 B2**: A preserves its inset composition through empty and five-digit-file views, whereas B's find captures switch from the padded 03 layout to flush prompts and 0/0 controls; A's empty find field also lacks a visible focus caret. *Change:* Keep a steady, fine focus caret visible when A's find query is empty.

## muse

A is better in every scene, in both themes, because one inset grid governs tabs, gutter, text, panels and minimap, whereas B is the same editor with that proportion removed: labels touch the glass, shortcuts clip, and character brackets and literal prefixes do the work of UI in the palette and find states. Neither side is publishable: they share an anonymous monospace set with merely adequate leading, an off-the-shelf pastel syntax palette, a minimap that alternates between stray boxed thumbnail and striped barcode, and a crude block caret with no visible current-line or hover step. A is a polished commercial product with one disciplined frame; B shows what that frame was holding back. Top gaps, ranked: first, the saturated block caret and indistinct secondary states; second, the bordered striped minimap that competes with the page instead of receding; third, default-feeling typography and an uneven tonal ladder in which comments are not clearly separated from gutter numerals.

- **C1 A3 B3**: In the 1:1 code crops both sides show the same crisp but anonymous wide monospace glyphs at the same size and merely adequate line pitch, with only the left offset differing *Change:* Reset code and chrome in a higher-quality monospace drawn for this size, on an integer pixel pitch with slightly more leading
- **C2 A3 B3**: Both sides keep body text brightest and gutter numerals dimmer, but code comments sit in a mid grey close to the gutter step and the large-file minimap column is the strongest texture on the right *Change:* Rebuild the neutral ladder at perceptually even steps, lifting comments to at least 4.5:1 and dropping the minimap one step below the gutter
- **C3 A3 B3**: Both sides use the same cool neutrals, single blue accent and pastel purple, teal, green and orange syntax, tidy but recognisably off-the-shelf in the code crops *Change:* Reduce syntax colour to at most five equal-lightness low-chroma hues and reserve the blue accent solely for caret, focus and current match
- **C4 A3 B2**: A aligns tab labels, gutter, text, panel input and right-hand counts on inset columns, while B puts the first tab, line numbers, panel text and shortcuts hard against or clipped by the window edges *Change:* Suppress the boxed minimap thumbnail when the whole file is visible so the frame contains no object without a job
- **C5 A3 B2**: In light scenes both active tabs read as a white shape on a pale strip, but in dark scenes the active label is only brighter text in fixed wide slots and the hover-tab pair shows no visible hover step, with B also touching the left edge *Change:* Give the dark active tab the same page-continuous tonal shape as the light theme and set label padding optically rather than in fixed slots
- **C6 A3 B2**: A right-aligns dim numerals in a clear measure even at five digits in the large scene, whereas B starts five-digit numerals at the glass edge, and neither side lifts the current line numeral *Change:* Lift the current line numeral one tonal step while leaving all other numerals at the present dim level
- **C7 A2 B2**: For short files both sides draw a small bordered grey thumbnail at top right, and for the large file both become a striped vertical barcode whose pale viewport box is wider than the map, with B clipped at the right edge *Change:* Remove the minimap border, lower its texture contrast below all text, and make the viewport a same-width soft tonal fill
- **C8 A3 B2**: A sets find and palette as one inset bottom line with a worded count, while B in palette and hover or empty find adds literal Command and Find prefixes, bracket characters around toggles, and right-edge-clipped shortcuts *Change:* Spell the find toggles as quiet muted words aligned with the count instead of cryptic abbreviations and symbols
- **C9 A2 B2**: Both sides mark insertion with the same solid saturated blue block swallowing the glyph beneath it and show a pale find-match tint, but no current-line step or tab hover step is visible *Change:* Replace the block caret with a fine exact bar in the existing caret pixel region
- **C10 A3 B2**: A keeps an intact blank page in the empty scene, but both sides leave a short stray hairline at top right of an empty file above a vast uncomposed void, with B adding a bracketed 0/0 find-empty state *Change:* Draw no minimap or hairline for empty and fully visible files, leaving a calm blank page with tab and caret only

## opus

The two sides share one face, one palette and one minimap, so they differ in proportion alone, and there A wins outright. A sets tabs, text and the find field on a single inset column with air under the strip. B removes every margin: labels and line numbers touch the window edge, numbers abut the code, and B's find and palette fall back to a terminal prompt ('Find:', 'Command:') and bracket characters ('[ ] [aa] [ ] 1/1'), which is the rubric's 1-anchor. The 03-find captures are pixel-identical between the sides, so that scene is a tie. B's own find chrome shows only in scenes 10 and 11. A is a competent 3 across the board but nothing an editor would publish. The three gaps keeping it out of the pages, in order: (1) the caret is a crude full-cell block where a fine accent bar belongs; (2) the type is the anonymous DejaVu, not a face of real quality set as the design; (3) the frame has no single proportion system (the active tab is marked only by tone in dark, the current line number is not lifted, and a minimap stub is left in the empty state). All three fixes cost 0 B or one font blob, with no extra dirty pixels per keystroke.

- **C1 A3 B2**: Both sides set the same DejaVu Sans Mono at the same size and pitch with clean stems in the 1:1 crops; B pushes the code to x=48 with the line numbers one space from it, so the gutter reads as part of the text ('10000Paragraph'), while A leaves a clear gap and a 32 px inset. *Change:* A: replace the anonymous face with a face of real quality (the council's IBM Plex Mono pick) at its drawn size, and keep the whole-pixel line pitch.
- **C2 A3 B3**: The tokens are identical: text, then grey comments ('// stop early'), then dim line numbers, then a near-invisible minimap, in both themes on both sides; B's flush gutter merges tonally with the first code column. *Change:* A: lift the current line's number one step and drop the minimap texture one more step, so the ladder runs text > comment > gutter > map > hairline in even OKLCH steps.
- **C3 A3 B3**: The palette is the same on both sides: a cool-tinted near-black and a lavender paper, one blue accent for the caret and selection, and four low-chroma syntax hues (violet, teal, ochre, sage); light is not a plain inversion. *Change:* A: bring the ochre and teal syntax hues to equal OKLCH lightness with the violet, so no hue jumps forward ('LIMIT' and 'dot' do now).
- **C4 A3 B1**: A insets the tabs, text and find field on one 48 px column, with a row of air under the strip; B sets 'hello.c', the line numbers, 'Find:' and the palette shortcuts flush against the window edges, and the minimap touches the corner. *Change:* A: derive every margin (the 48 px text column, the strip height, the minimap inset and the bottom bar) from one unit, so the top air and the right margin match the left.
- **C5 A3 B2**: A's light active tab is a white slab continuous with the page and its labels start on the text column; B's strip starts at x=0 with 'hello.c' touching the edge, and in the dark theme the active tab on both sides is marked only by a brighter label. *Change:* A: give the dark active tab the same page-continuous slab as the light theme, and space the labels optically by width, not on fixed 128 px pitches.
- **C6 A3 B1**: A right-aligns dim numbers in a measured gutter with a gap before the code, and the current line is not lifted; B's numbers sit at x=0 and abut the code ('9 static', '10000Paragraph'). *Change:* A: lift the current line's number one tonal step.
- **C7 A3 B2**: On both sides the map is a quiet thumbnail with a soft grey viewport box (big.md, y≈485-550); on large files it shows as a striped column. A insets it from the top and right, and B jams it into the top-right corner. *Change:* A: draw the large-file map as a faithful miniature rather than uniform stripes, and align its top with the first text row.
- **C8 A3 B1**: A's find is a single line: the field on the text column, quiet 'Aa ab .*' toggles and '1 of 1'; its palette shortcuts end on the same right edge. B prints a terminal prompt 'Find:' and 'Command:', bracket characters '[ ] [aa] [ ] 1/1' and '0/0', and shortcuts touching the window edge. *Change:* A: set the palette shortcuts in tabular figures on a fixed column and draw toggle state by tone, not by the ad-hoc underline under 'ab'.
- **C9 A2 B2**: On both sides the caret is a full-cell blue block over the glyph ('#', '/', 'P'), the match is a soft blue tint, and no current line is drawn, so the states are distinct but the caret is crude. *Change:* A: replace the cell block with a 2 px (at 192 DPI) accent bar in the caret pixel region.
- **C10 A3 B2**: A's empty window shows 'untitled', '1' and the caret on the inset column, but a stray hairline floats where the minimap would be (x≈1576-1640, y≈31); B shows the same hairline, everything flush to the edges, and an orphaned '1' at x=0. *Change:* A: hide the minimap stub in an empty or one-line buffer.
