## codex

Ranking: IBM Plex Mono 2.5.0 Regular > DejaVu Sans Mono (today) > JetBrains Mono 2.304 Regular > Commit Mono 1.143 Regular > Iosevka (custom build, regular).

**Choice.** F3

I would choose F3 at 28px em on a 36px pitch. Its open forms, measured lightness, and distinctive letter feet give sublimité an authored voice across prose, code, and tab labels. F5 is the quieter runner-up, but F3 makes the type itself carry more of the design. Neither earns the severe 5/5. Scores assume the proposed tuning; those sizes need fresh pixel proofs, and the unseen find-control string is inferred from the displayed glyphs. Designer-intended optical size cannot be established from these images.

- **JetBrains Mono 2.304 Regular** (C1 3): Clean stems, substantial lowercase, and rounded rectangular bowls produce an even but assertive colour. The dotted zero, hooked l, brackets, and commas distinguish code reliably. At the shown 36px pitch, prose feels separated into individual rows. Tab names are clear; the same substantial forms would make find controls slightly emphatic. Accomplished, but its voice remains predominantly technical. *Size: At 192 DPI, proof 28px em (10.5pt) on a 34px baseline pitch. Preserve the face’s natural advance; add no tracking.*
- **Commit Mono 1.143 Regular** (C1 3): Even strokes and softly curved terminals give prose an approachable voice. The slashed zero distinguishes numbers clearly; braces and semicolons remain readable in code. Its shown 29px pitch compresses paragraphs and punctuation into a busy block. Tab labels carry the face comfortably, though repeated dots, stars, and digits would make find controls feel more utilitarian than finely composed. *Size: At 192 DPI, proof 28px em (10.5pt) on a 34px baseline pitch. Extra leading is the essential correction.*
- **IBM Plex Mono 2.5.0 Regular** (C1 4): The strongest editorial character: broad bowls, open counters, and pronounced i/l feet make the grid feel deliberately drawn. Its lighter colour and shown 35px pitch carry the quotation and README particularly well. Dotted zeros and distinct brackets preserve code clarity. Tab names feel composed; find labels should share that poise. Delicate punctuation and conspicuous letter feet introduce small interruptions in colour, keeping it below 5. *Size: At 192 DPI, proof 28px em (10.5pt) on a 36px baseline pitch. Retain the natural width and regular weight.*
- **Iosevka (custom build, regular)** (C1 3): Tall, narrow bowls and closely repeated vertical strokes create a distinctly compressed rhythm. The shown 14px advance saves space, but prose and tab names look squeezed beside their generous height. Slashed zeros and clearly articulated braces serve code well. Periods, asterisks, and narrow numerals would make find controls visually uneven. Enlarging helps readability; the condensed proportions remain the governing character. *Size: At 192 DPI, proof 30px em (11.25pt) on a 36px baseline pitch. Let the natural advance increase with size; avoid horizontal stretching.*
- **DejaVu Sans Mono (today)** (C1 4): The most balanced neutral option: open lowercase, steady strokes, and restrained terminals give prose and code a consistent, moderately dark colour. The dotted zero, clear 1, and firm punctuation support scanning without dominating it. Tab names already look settled; Aa, ab, dots, stars, and counts should form coherent controls. The shown 31px pitch is slightly tight for sustained prose. Refined, though less distinctive than F3. *Size: At 192 DPI, proof 28px em (10.5pt) on a 34px baseline pitch. Keep regular weight and natural spacing.*

## muse

Ranking: JetBrains Mono 2.304 Regular > IBM Plex Mono 2.5.0 Regular > DejaVu Sans Mono (today) > Commit Mono 1.143 Regular > Iosevka (custom build, regular).

**Choice.** F1

Choose F1: across all four quadrants it best balances clarity for code (crisp syntax colors, distinct symbols/numbers) with comfortable prose rhythm (uncluttered word shapes, well-spaced lines, clean diacritics/quotes). It wins by a small margin over F3, mainly for a slightly more open horizontal feel that helps long identifiers and mixed prose/code reading.

- **JetBrains Mono 2.304 Regular** (C1 4): F1 dark code is the easiest to read: even light-purple (keyword/type) vs white (identifiers) vs orange (numbers) separation; code lines are short/clean; F1 light whitespace keeps words distinct (no cramping) and the "minimap" highlight reads clearly. Cap-height/x-height feels balanced (not overly wide/narrow), so both code density and README prose flow well. *Size: Keep ~11.5–12.5pt (≈15–16px) at 96–110 DPI; line-height 1.45–1.55 (≈22–24px) to preserve the airy rhythm it already has. It tolerates slightly tighter leading than the others without losing clarity.*
- **Commit Mono 1.143 Regular** (C1 3): F2 looks similar but darker/heavier weight makes the dark screen feel denser: the code is legible yet less “airy” (slightly bolder stems). In light mode it packs many more lines (tighter line-height) which helps scan a README quickly but slightly reduces word-shape breathing room for long prose paragraphs. *Size: Use a touch smaller than F1 (~11–12pt) with a bit more line-height (1.5–1.6) to offset the heavier weight; if you keep current tight leading, drop size by ~0.5pt to avoid a crowded feel.*
- **IBM Plex Mono 2.5.0 Regular** (C1 4): F3 is a very close second: dark code has clean, slightly narrower glyphs with clear distinction of 0/O and punctuation; light README has generous line spacing and crisp diacritics (é, »…«) that stay sharp. It reads marginally “tighter” horizontally than F1, making long identifiers a fraction more compact but not cramped. *Size: Aim ~11.5–12pt with line-height 1.5–1.55; keep its native spacious leading—it benefits long prose. Avoid reducing line-height below 1.4 or the narrow forms will feel squeezed.*
- **Iosevka (custom build, regular)** (C1 3): F4 is noticeably more condensed and lighter: the dark code is thinner and slightly less punchy at distance (lower perceived contrast), though symbol shapes are distinct. The light README is pleasant and compact, fitting many lines, but the lighter weight can tire eyes faster in extended dark-mode coding. *Size: Bump size up (~12–12.5pt) and keep line-height moderate (1.45–1.5). The condensed width means you gain horizontal space, so a half-point increase costs little.*
- **DejaVu Sans Mono (today)** (C1 3): F5 dark code is solid and friendly (rounded-ish forms, clear numbers), very readable, though a hair less refined in spacing rhythm than F1/F3 (some letter pairs feel a touch uneven). Light README is comfortable and even, with well-formed punctuation and quotes; overall reliable but a fraction more “neutral” than distinctive. *Size: ~11.5–12pt, line-height 1.5. It's forgiving—works well from 1.4 to 1.6 leading—so tune pitch to match your grid rather than the face.*

## opus

Ranking: IBM Plex Mono 2.5.0 Regular > JetBrains Mono 2.304 Regular > Commit Mono 1.143 Regular > Iosevka (custom build, regular) > DejaVu Sans Mono (today).

**Choice.** F3

I would choose F3. It is the only one of the five that makes the editor read like a finely set page instead of a terminal. Its slab serifs and calligraphic braces give prose a warm, even colour and a strong horizontal line (compare the README paragraphs at lines 29–33 against F1's and F4's), while code keeps every distinction it needs: correct „ » « quotes and em dash, a centred asterisk, a clear zero, distinct backticks and brackets. Because the whole interface is this one face, tab names and the `Aa ab .*  1 of 1` find line would inherit that literary voice, which is the 'one voice, like a book' the 5 describes. F1 is a very close second. It is technically the strongest code face and has the best current leading (36 px), but its tall x-height and blunt shapes read as tooling rather than a periodical. F2 has refined shapes but is choked by 29 px leading and is faint in light mode. F4 is condensed, with a footnote-height asterisk. F5 is the default fallback face and currently drops underscores from the screen. Set F3 at a 16 px advance (26.7 px em) and open the pitch from 35 to 37–38 px (about 1.4 em); that one deliberate leading change is what would take it from 4 to 5.

- **JetBrains Mono 2.304 Regular** (C1 4): Cells are 16 px wide with a 36 px pitch (about 1.35 em), a whole number of pixels. That gives generous, even leading and the calmest vertical rhythm of the five. The x-height is tall and the counters open; stems are crisp in both themes with no fringes. Light-theme text is a firm, even dark grey. Punctuation is complete and correct: „ » « — and the German quote line all set properly. The asterisk is centred and works as a multiplication sign, 0/O and 1/l/I are clearly told apart, and backticks and brackets are distinct. Code is excellent. Prose is clear but reads as an engineered instrument rather than a book: the big x-height and blunt, squared terminals make paragraphs a little loud and wide-set. Tab labels (hello.c, util.py) and a find line like `Aa ab .*  1 of 1` would be very legible and share one voice with the text. It is a face of real quality, but its voice is technical rather than literary. *Size: Keep the 16 px advance (26.7 px em) and the 36 px pitch. If you want more columns or a quieter page, drop to 15 px advance (25 px em) with a 34 px pitch, which keeps about 1.36 em. Do not go below 1.3 em because the tall x-height needs the air.*
- **Commit Mono 1.143 Regular** (C1 3): Same 16 px cell, but the pitch is only 29 px (about 1.09 em). Descenders on one line nearly meet the ascenders and caps on the next (lines 8–11 and 29–33 of the README). Paragraphs turn into a dense grey slab and the eye loses the line, so the leading is the weakest of the five. The face itself is a refined neo-grotesque with a light, even colour and a slashed zero. It is pleasant in code on dark, but its thin stems make the light-theme block look washed out next to F1 and F3. The opening „ on the German quote line renders as a small, ambiguous mark that reads almost like a guillemet; that is the one punctuation weak spot. Chrome labels would be elegant but faint at this weight. *Size: Keep the 16 px advance and open the pitch to 36–37 px (1.35–1.4 em). Use the Regular weight, not a light or book cut. With that leading it could reach a solid 3+, but the face is still the most anonymous of the good ones.*
- **IBM Plex Mono 2.5.0 Regular** (C1 4): 16 px cell and 35 px pitch (about 1.31 em), a whole number with deliberate, even leading. The slab serifs on i, l, r and the soft, calligraphic curly braces give the block a typewriter-book texture. The README is the only render that reads like a set page rather than a terminal: lines 29–33 have an even, warm colour and a strong horizontal flow along the slabs. Stems are crisp in both themes. Quotes „ » « and the em dash are correct and well drawn, 0 is dotted or slashed, and the asterisk is centred. The slanted hash (`##`, `#include`) and the shaped braces add character without noise. Code stays clear, and string and comment lines hold their colour. Tab names and a find line like `Aa ab .*  1 of 1` would carry the same literary voice as the document, which is what 'chrome and code share one voice' asks for. It falls short of 5 only on leading: 1.31 em is adequate, not yet the generous measure a book-like mono wants. *Size: Keep the 16 px advance (26.7 px em). Raise the pitch to 37–38 px (about 1.4 em); slab monos breathe best there, and it is the change most likely to lift this to a 5. Do not shrink the size: the moderate x-height needs the full 26–27 px em.*
- **Iosevka (custom build, regular)** (C1 3): A condensed face: about 14 px advance, so the 75-character comment ends near x=1104 where the others end near 1262. Pitch is 32 px (about 1.2 em). The block is dense and tall-narrow; prose reads squeezed, with the urgent, ticker-like texture of a terminal. The multiplication asterisk is raised like a footnote mark (`a.x * b.x`), which is wrong for code. The percent signs in `%s %d` look stylised and fussy. The zero has a dot, and underscores and brackets are clear. Stems are crisp. Its density is useful but has no book quality, and narrow tab labels would look cramped beside the wide gaps between them. *Size: Raise the size until the advance is a whole 15 px (about 28–30 px em, depending on its unit width). Set the pitch to 34–35 px (about 1.2 em of the new size), keeping the pitch a whole number.*
- **DejaVu Sans Mono (today)** (C1 1): 16 px cell and 31 px pitch (about 1.16 em). The underscores are missing on screen: line 1 shows `design shots.sh` and line 7 shows `int32 t x`, because the low underscore is clipped or overpainted by the next row. That is wrong text on screen. The letterforms are the standard Linux fallback sans mono. It is legible and even, but anonymous, and it reads as a face chosen by default, which the rubric scores 1. The prose block is a uniform, mechanical grey with no voice. *Size: Not recommended. If it were kept, the pitch would need to be at least 34 px so the underscore clears the cell, but that does not fix the default-face problem.*
