# Title font for *Sublimité*

Research note, 2026-10-09. Scope: the title lettering only (logo, about box, README/site header,
empty-state title). Editing text stays in the monospace cell grid. Samples are in this directory;
`all.png` is the contact sheet (ranked order), `accents.png` compares the `é` and `!` at 300 px.

## 1. Reading the "Zeitalter der Ausrufe"

Caveat: the full text of „Meine Erlebnisse im Zeitalter der Ausrufe" (*Lieblose Legenden*, DVA 1952,
*Ges. Werke* I, p. 72) is not online. What follows is a reading of the brief's key line and of
the collection, not of a close reading of the story.

The satire is aimed at the 1950s culture business, but the gesture it mocks is older: an educated
German public that no longer states anything, gazes deep into each other's eyes and exclaims *in
French*. That is the Bildungsbürger's salon effusiveness of 1890–1910: the *Jugend* and *Pan* years,
art as a cause for rapture, the Böcklin print over the sofa. Typographically the age of exclamation
is the age when **letters themselves gesture**: Jugendstil display faces made for posters and
mastheads, where every stem swells and every terminal flicks. Three registers fit:

- **A. German Jugendstil display** (Eckmann 1900, Böcklin 1904, Feder-Antiqua 1911): the exact
  milieu. The letters swell and flick. They read as a period quotation, which suits an ironic name.
- **B. Didone salon / fashion italic** (Bodoni, Didot): French, high contrast, and in italic it
  leans in like someone gazing into your eyes. It also fits the postwar reading, since Didot was
  the face of 1950s *Harper's Bazaar*/*Vogue*, the culture industry Hildesheimer mocked.
- **C. Secession / Reklameschrift** (Vienna 1898–1910, German advertising type): sober geometry with
  a poster's shout. This is the "advertising exclamation" reading.

**Pick: A.** The joke of the name is that the editor states and its title exclaims. A Jugendstil
masthead next to a bare monospace grid makes that contrast readable at a glance, and it belongs to
the German salon that Hildesheimer's French exclamation points at. **Strongest alternative: B**, a Bodoni
italic. It is more refined and more legible, but the joke is quieter and it reads as "luxury brand".

## 3. Ranked top 3 and wildcard

**1. CAT Eckmann** (`cat-eckmann.png`). Otto Eckmann drew lettering for *Jugend*, the Munich weekly
that gave the movement its name and was its most enthusiastic voice, and Eckmann-Schrift (1900) was
the typeface of German art-rapture around 1900. The letters are brush-written and swell like a voice
rising: the `S` coils, `b l t` taper into flicks, and the `!` is one confident brush stroke. Set as
`Sublimité` it looks like a 1900 masthead, which is the right ironic register for a product that
otherwise says nothing loud. It is heavy, so it survives small sizes and dark backgrounds. Wiegel's
digitisation is clean at 300 px (`accents.png`) and complete: 482 glyphs with kerning and German/French
punctuation.

**2. Bodoni Moda Italic, opsz 96** (`bodoni-moda-italic.png`). This is the salon register: hairlines,
ball terminals and a forward lean, the „tief in die Augen" moment. It has the best pedigree in register B
and the cleanest licence (no Reserved Font Name, so a static, subset instance can ship under any name).
Its weakness is that the hairlines vanish below about 60 px at opsz 96, so small uses should take
`opsz` equal to the pixel size. Choose it if Tobias reads the era as French elegance rather than German
Jugendstil effusion.

**3. Böcklins Universe** (`boecklins-universe.png`). This is the most exclamatory face of all, with
floral swash `S`, curled `t` and `é` grown into its accent. It is named after the painter whose *Toteninsel*
hung over every bourgeois sofa, which is a fine joke. It ranks third because of its 1970s
head-shop revival (the CorelDRAW "Arabia" clone): to many eyes it reads as kitsch rather than as a period
quotation. Its file is also large (753 KB).

**Wildcard: Federant** (`federant.png`). It revives Nägele's 1911 *Reklameschrift*: advertising type,
the typography of public exclamation. It has a strange reversed-swash `S`, spare and slightly comic.
It is closest to the "Feuilleton/advertising" reading, and at 44 KB it is tiny.

## 4. Recommendation

**Use CAT Eckmann for the wordmark. The runner-up is Bodoni Moda Italic.** If the wordmark needs a
quieter companion (e.g. a subtitle line in the about box), Bodoni Moda Italic pairs well with it.

## 5. The `é` (it is in the logo)

- **CAT Eckmann**: a separate, heavy teardrop acute that leans right and echoes the stem flicks. It sits
  very close to the bowl of the `e`; at ≤ 24 px the two touch. For the logo, keep ≥ 48 px or draw
  the wordmark once as outlines and open the gap by 2–3 % of the em by hand.
- **Bodoni Moda Italic**: a long, thin wedge, well clear of the `e`, very French. Its tail is a hairline that
  disappears on dark backgrounds at small sizes.
- **Böcklins Universe**: the accent is a swash grown out of the letter. It is the most decorative and is unmistakable.
- **Federant / Playfair 2 Italic**: a clean wedge accent with no problems.
Always use precomposed U+00E9 (all candidates map it), never `e` + U+0301.

## 6. What to embed and ship

Preferred route: **bake the wordmark at build time** (outlines → SVG for README/site, a coverage
bitmap or path list for the about box). Images made with an OFL font are not covered by the OFL, so
the binary carries artwork, not a font. Credit "Title lettering: CAT Eckmann by Peter Wiegel (OFL)"
anyway.

If live text in the title face is needed, embed the **unmodified** file:

- `third_party/fonts/cat-eckmann/CAT-Eckmann.ttf` (285 KB, sha256 `e5b1adee…6dfe4b27`)
- `third_party/fonts/cat-eckmann/OFL.txt` = `Open Font License.txt` from `Eckmann.zip`
  (sha256 `431db9de…14c8c4707e`; header: © 2018 Peter Wiegel, RFN "CAT-Eckmann")
- Source: http://www.peter-wiegel.de/Fonts/Eckmann.zip (zip sha256 `6621bff1…c1bc8d`)

Do **not** subset or set `fsType` under the name "CAT-Eckmann". Under the OFL that is a Modified Version and the
Reserved Font Name would have to change (e.g. "Sublimite Title"), unless Wiegel agrees. Runner-up
files: `BodoniModa-Italic[opsz,wght].ttf` + `OFL.txt` from google/fonts. A static `opsz=96, wght=400`
instance (≈ 60 KB) can be shipped freely because there is no RFN.

## 7. Licence doubts and near misses

- **Wiegel metadata is inconsistent.** The name-table copyright says "All rights reserved" (Eckmann,
  Böcklins Universe), but each zip ships the full OFL 1.1 text with an explicit copyright line and RFN, and
  Eckmann's licence field says "This Font is under SIL Open Font License". The shipped OFL file governs.
  CAT Eckmann also has `fsType = 4` (preview & print), which conflicts with the OFL's terms. `fsType` is
  advisory and the OFL permits embedding, but a short confirmation e-mail to wiegel@peter-wiegel.de
  would close this. Barlösius and Secession are dual GPL+FE / OFL; take the OFL.
- **Auriol** (Georges Auriol, Peignot 1901), the ideal *French* Art Nouveau face, has only
  Linotype/Monotype versions (Carter 1979), which are commercial. Ivan Louette's free "George A Rebours"
  and the "Krondor" clone have no stated licence, so they are rejected.
- **Arnold Böcklin / Eckmann**: the Linotype, URW, Bitstream (Freeform 710/721) and Paper Moon cuts are commercial.
  Dieter Steffmann's Eckmann-Schrift and Rudelsberg are "freeware" with no OFL, so they are rejected.
- **Excoffon's Mistral, Choc, Banco** (Fonderie Olive 1951–55), the real 1950s French exclamation faces, have
  no open version. **Theano Didot** (OFL Didot revival) qualified on paper, but the CTAN download failed
  and it was not rendered.

Renders were made with pango-view 1.52 (HarfBuzz shaping, unhinted) against a fontconfig restricted to
the candidates. Variable fonts were instanced with fontTools 4.62. Della Respira's `„` is a fallback glyph from another
candidate.
